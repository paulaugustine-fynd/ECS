# Coach UAE — presenter’s walkthrough

## Start here

Open `/login?next=/operator/coach` on the demo. Sign in with **admin@ati.demo** and **Demo123!**, then choose **Start Coach UAE journey**. Use fictional details only.

This is one saved story with 37 steps. Allow about 35–45 minutes when explaining it. Keep the supplied values for the first presentation. Each page tells you **who acts**, **what to do** and **what should happen**. Follow the main button; when responsibility changes, choose **Open Coach partner page** or **Open ATI review page**.

The partner and ATI pages are separate. The partner pages use the Bloomingdale’s layout; ATI pages use the Fynd-style console. You remain signed in as the presenter: switching pages demonstrates responsibilities, not real account impersonation. Coach is a fictional sample partner, not a registered real Coach business.

Progress is saved after each successful action. To resume, return to `/operator/coach` using the same login and choose a saved journey. Starting another journey does not erase previous presentations. Do not use browser Back to repeat a completed action: use the chapter list to inspect it.

## One complete workflow, beginning to end

### 1. Join: invitation, application and review

1. **ATI — Invite Coach UAE.** Choose **Create sample invitation**. The email is a private preview, not a sent message.
2. **Coach — Complete your business application.** Open the partner page. Review the fictional company/contact details and three sample documents. Choose **Submit application**. The **العربية** button demonstrates Arabic application labels.
3. **ATI — Ask for a document correction.** Explain that the sample licence needs an updated expiry. Choose **Send correction request**. The reason is saved.
4. **Coach — Replace the requested document.** See ATI’s reason, attach the sample replacement and choose **Send corrected application**. The first version remains in history.
5. **ATI — Approve the business and team access.** Review the corrected version and choose **Approve Coach UAE**. A simulated finance-business reference is recorded. Approval is not permission to start selling yet.

### 2. Prepare: terms, selling permission and locations

6. **ATI Finance — Set commercial and return terms.** Save the illustrative bag/wallet commissions, minimum bag price, statement schedule, 30-day return window, seven extra receipt days, shipping and return charges. These are demo assumptions, not actual Coach terms or tax advice.
7. **ATI — Approve Coach selling permission.** Record the sample authorisation for Coach bags and wallets in the UAE.
8. **ATI Operations — Add the fulfilment locations.** Prepare the Coach warehouse, Coach store and ATI warehouse. Registration responses are simulated.

### 3. Products: import, correct, review and price

9. **Coach — Bring in the Coach collection.** Choose Excel, CSV, API, SFTP, Shopify or WooCommerce and an image source. Each selection uses a labelled sample feed; it does not open a real connector. Receive six sample rows.
10. **Coach — Fix the file before reviewing products.** Exclude the duplicate and match “shade” to “Colour”. Save the prices. Five distinct products remain, including colour options. Arabic content still needs review.
11. **ATI Catalogue — Review product and lifestyle images.** Approve sample-asset use and reject the poor-image example. The product drawings and creative-job response are fixtures, not real Coach photography or a live image generator.
12. **ATI Arabic Reviewer — Review English and Arabic names.** Edit the supplied bilingual example and approve it. Empty Arabic content is blocked.
13. **ATI Catalogue — Approve the collection.** Below-threshold trusted products can follow the automatic route. Higher-value bags need the explicit manual-review confirmation.
14. **ATI Pricing — Review prices and launch promotion.** The default bag price becomes AED 1,620 after the 10% partner-funded discount. Below-minimum prices require a recorded exception.

### 4. Launch: publish, stock, preview and activate

15. **ATI Catalogue — Send approved products to connected systems.** Publish to the business-record, order-management and storefront simulators. Optional: expand **Try a failed update**, interrupt it, refresh the page and choose **Try this update again**. Failure must not silently publish products.
16. **Coach — Set stock and keep a safety buffer.** Use 12 units and a two-unit buffer per sample product. Stock is saved, but customer availability remains zero while Coach is inactive.
17. **ATI Catalogue — Preview what the customer sees.** Check all five product cards: names, prices and approved illustrations. The customer-facing seller is Bloomingdale’s, not the supplier.
18. **ATI Operations — Check a sample order before opening sales.** Choose **Run safe launch check**. The virtual check does not consume sellable stock.
19. **ATI — Open Coach UAE for sales.** Review the checklist and activate. Missing preparation checks block activation. Available quantities are now shown.

### 5. Sell and deliver: warehouse, orders, pause and collection

20. **ATI Warehouse — Replenish the ATI warehouse.** Book a replacement slot after the sample slot conflict and receive five units. The purchase order, appointment and stock receipt stay linked.
21. **ATI Operations — Receive a customer basket.** Order two Coach bags, one Coach wallet and one other-partner item. The basket creates three deliveries. Try 99 bags to show the stock check, then return to two. The chosen routing preference is recorded; this small fixture uses the stocked sample locations rather than a general optimisation engine.
22. **Coach — Pick and pack the Coach parcels.** Confirm two bags and one wallet. The other partner’s parcel is not shown in this task. Wrong quantities are rejected.
23. **ATI — Pause new sales without losing open orders.** Pause Coach and one product. New availability becomes zero, while the packed order stays valid.
24. **ATI Operations — Dispatch and deliver the packed order.** Record simulated tracking and delivery. Each parcel has its own state and reference. No real courier is booked.
25. **ATI — Reopen Coach sales.** Remaining stock returns to sale; delivered quantities do not reappear.
26. **Coach Store — Collect a separate order in store.** Enter **4826**. Try a wrong code first if useful: stock must remain unchanged. Correct collection removes one cardholder from stock.

### 6. Service: explain a delay

27. **ATI Operations — Handle a late shipment warning.** Compare the sample 52-hour wait with a 24-hour deadline. Save the owner, private recipient and resolution. This separate late-order example does not undo the delivered order. Notifications are previews, not emails.

### 7. After purchase: return, refund and exchange

28. **ATI Customer Service — Check a return request.** Request one bag at day 20. It links to the original delivered purchase. Day 31 is blocked with the default 30-day policy.
29. **ATI Operations — Arrange the return collection.** Save a sample pickup window and label reference. No courier is actually dispatched.
30. **Coach Returns — Inspect the returned bag.** Record receipt on day 24 and choose **Good** for the main story. The unit returns to sellable stock. **Damaged** keeps it unavailable; the next refund then needs an ATI exception.
31. **ATI Finance — Approve the refund and partner adjustment.** Refund the original paid price, AED 1,620, with a commission reversal and AED 20 handling charge. A damaged item stays unavailable even if its refund is approved.
32. **ATI Customer Service — Exchange the wallet for another colour.** Refund the original wallet and create a separate linked replacement at AED 700. Keep payment **Approved** for the main story. **Declined** demonstrates that the replacement does not ship or consume stock; the completed original refund remains recorded.

### 8. Settle and review: statement, Finance and reporting

33. **ATI Finance — Review Coach’s statement.** Calculate and lock the statement from this journey’s actual saved sales and returns. With the default values, sales after refunds are **AED 2,670**, and shipping plus return charges are **AED 35**. The net amount also deducts the illustrative tax and commission. The other partner’s sale is not included in Coach’s statement.
34. **ATI Finance — Send the statement to Finance.** Save a simulated receipt for the same locked amount. No bank payment is made.
35. **ATI Finance — Compare expected and received amounts.** Show a conversion-rate difference and optional import-tax adjustment. Save them separately; do not rewrite the locked UAE statement.
36. **ATI Analyst — Review results and share the report.** Save the private report preview and intended recipient. Nothing is emailed. Download statement entries, sample packing information or the complete saved record from **Saved business records**.
37. **ATI Administrator — Explore another market and business.** Preview Saudi Arabia or Kuwait settings and another business name. This is a settings demonstration, not a new live tenant or a scale test. The UAE journey remains unchanged.

Open **Workbook checklist** to see all 54 requirement numbers, plain-English descriptions and the steps that demonstrate them. Completed steps remain available for inspection. This checklist measures demonstration coverage, not production acceptance.

## Shorter workflows

### Registering a new partner

Follow steps 1–5, then 6–19 to prepare the partner for sales. Registration, approval and activation are three different milestones. Being approved does not bypass the product, stock or safe-order checks.

### An existing partner’s daily work

Resume a saved journey. Use steps 9–14 for the collection, 16 for stock, 22 for packing, 26 for collection and 30 for return inspection. Inspect completed steps without resubmitting them. Start a new journey to try different quantities or terms from the beginning.

### Reviewing as ATI

The main review points are steps 3, 5, 7, 11–14, 17–19 and 30–33. Read the submitted values, record a reason where requested, and act. Decisions remain in **What happened so far** and **Saved business records**. The Coach story uses a correction-and-approval path; arbitrary reject/reopen/offboard branches are in the separate operational partner workspace, not all reproduced in this guided story.

### Explaining system failure and recovery

At an external-update step, expand **Try a failed update**. Simulate the unavailable service. The step stays unfinished, business quantities remain unchanged, and the failed attempt is saved. Refresh and retry. Successful recovery advances the journey once. This is a simulated response, not evidence that live Fynd is connected.

## What the launch checks mean

In the Coach journey, **Ready** means the required earlier actions succeeded in the saved simulation. The server also checks the prerequisites when you activate; it is not just a decorative tick.

In the separate operational workspace, preparation checks inspect actual stored document reviews, effective agreements and selling rights, current simulated brand/location acknowledgements, current product publication, stock comparisons, storefront content and a safe test order. A green simulated check is **not** confirmation from a live Fynd, ERP or Bloomingdale’s website.

The previously confusing “before activation, availability is zero” message is now conditional: active partners are compared with remaining sellable stock, inactive partners with zero availability. Existing old records were not silently marked ready just to remove warnings.

## What works, what remains simulated, and what is not complete

- **Working software:** sign-in, server checks, database-saved journeys, separate page layouts, forms, stock arithmetic, price controls, returns, statement calculations, history, recovery and downloads.
- **Simulated by design:** all external business systems, invitations, translation responses, generated-image responses, delivery/pickup actions, customer payments/refunds and Finance receipts. No real credentials are needed.
- **Simplified for this story:** five sample products, fixed sample location choices, bundled import/documents, selected review paths and partial Arabic application copy. This is not arbitrary supplier-file processing, general AI translation/image generation, fully bilingual UI, or a production routing engine.
- **Not proved by this demo:** live Fynd compatibility, real supplier connectivity, production scale/security certification, real tax compliance, all commercial/promotion policy variants or every edge case in the requirements. The original implementation-evidence register remains honest about these gaps.
- **Separate data:** completing Coach steps does not create or alter native Partner/Product/Order rows. Those existing operational screens and their older sample records remain unchanged. Do not use them to look for the Coach journey’s order; use **Saved business records** inside Coach.

All 54 workbook rows have a demonstration mapping. That is deliberately not a claim that every detail of all 54 requirements has been natively implemented or that all possible bugs are gone.
